## 1. Brand fact sheet

**Company:** RK World Infocom Private Limited
**HQ:** Koramangala Industrial Layout, Bengaluru, Karnataka
**Incorporated:** 2 June 2008
**Origin story:** Began July 2003 as a Nokia handset distributorship under the
proprietorship of Mr. Ramesh Kumar Shah
**Parent:** RK Group — 40+ years across retail, distribution, e-commerce and import/export
**Positioning line in use:** *"Elevating Businesses, Enriching Choices: Your Path
to Quality and Savings"*

### What the business actually does

RK World Infocom is an **e-commerce distribution and marketplace-enablement
company**. Two things sit under one roof:

1. **FMCG online distribution** — an authorised FMCG seller on Amazon.in, moving
   Health & Personal Care, Grocery, Baby and Beauty products at national scale.
2. **Seller / brand enablement** — onboarding brands onto the marketplace:
   account setup, listing optimisation, inventory and fulfilment, analytics, and
   financial reconciliation.

**Post 3 — The category depth post**

> Four categories. Very different games.
>
> **Grocery** — thin margins, brutal velocity. You win on forecasting.

### Pillar 2 — Founder / origin story

**Post 4 — The pivot post** *(highest expected engagement)*

> In 2003, we sold Nokia phones.
>
> Not smartphones. The ones with the snake game and a month of battery life.
>
> By 2022, we were one of the largest FMCG sellers on India's biggest
> marketplace.
>
> Nobody plans that arc. What actually happened is simpler: the *product*
> changed completely, but the *capability* never did.
>
> Getting inventory from a manufacturer to a consumer, profitably, at scale,
> without breaking trust — that's the same job whether the box holds a handset
> or a shampoo bottle.
>
> Businesses that survive decades don't defend their product.
>
> They defend their capability, and let the product change.
>
> #Entrepreneurship #BusinessGrowth #Ecommerce #RKWorld

---

**Post 5 — The 40-year post**

> 40 years in distribution teaches you one uncomfortable thing:
>
> Every channel you master will eventually be disrupted by the next one.
>
> General trade. Modern trade. Marketplaces. Quick commerce.
>
> We've watched each one get called "the end" of the last. None of them were.

>
> The brands that struggled weren't the ones who were late to a channel.

### Pillar 3 — Category & market POV

**Post 6 — The authenticity post**

> The most expensive problem in Indian e-commerce isn't logistics.
>
> It's counterfeits.
>
> A brand spends 15 years building trust. One grey-market seller with a
> discounted listing undoes it in a quarter — and the one-star review doesn't
> say "fake seller." It says the brand didn't work.
>
> This is the entire argument for authorised distribution.
>
> Not control for its own sake. Control because the alternative is a brand
> paying for someone else's shortcut.
>
> #BrandProtection #Ecommerce #FMCG #Counterfeits

---

**Post 7 — The listicle**

> 5 things that kill a brand's first year on a marketplace:
>
> 1. **Launching the full catalogue at once.** 200 SKUs with no velocity data
>    means 200 chances to get forecasting wrong.
> 2. **Treating listings as data entry.** The title and first image *are* the
>    salesperson.
> 3. **No inventory buffer for a spike.** Going out of stock after a campaign
>    resets your ranking. You pay twice.
> 4. **Ignoring reconciliation.** Fees, returns and reimbursements quietly eat
>    margin that nobody audits.
> 5. **Pricing against the marketplace instead of against the channel mix.**
>    You end up competing with your own distributors.
>
> None of these are marketplace problems. All of them are preparation problems.
>
> #Ecommerce #BrandStrategy #AmazonSeller #FMCG

---

**Post 8 — The contrarian take**

> Unpopular opinion: most brands don't have a marketplace problem.
>
> They have a **margin visibility** problem.
>
> They know their landed cost. They know their selling price. They assume the
> gap is profit.
>
> Then you add referral fees, fulfilment fees, storage, returns processing,
> ad spend, and the promotion they ran in March that nobody reconciled.
>
> We've seen "profitable" SKUs that weren't. Not by a little.
>
> Before you optimise growth, audit the unit economics you already have.
> The fastest margin win is usually already inside your P&L.
>
> #Ecommerce #UnitEconomics #FMCG #Margins

**Rules of thumb**
- First 2 lines carry the post — LinkedIn truncates the rest.
- One idea per post. Split anything that needs two.
- 3–5 hashtags, at the end, not inline.
- No external link in the body; put it in the first comment.
- Reply to every comment in the first 90 minutes.
