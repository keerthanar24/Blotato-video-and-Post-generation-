# How to Write an AI Video Script That Doesn't Look AI-Generated
### The method + a worked 40-second example ("THE AGENT SPEAKS")

---

## Part 1 · Why most AI video scripts look the same

Feed a tool "make a video about our AI challenge" and you get the same six beats every time:
warm greeting → "in today's fast-paced world" → three benefit bullets → generic call to action.
It's not *wrong*. It's just **shaped like every other video**, so the eye slides off it.

Uniqueness doesn't come from better adjectives. It comes from **one structural decision made
before you write a word.** Here are the seven levers that actually move it.

---

## Part 2 · The seven levers

### 1. Pick a point of view that isn't "the company"
This is the single biggest lever. Same facts, different narrator = a completely different film.
For an AI-agent challenge you could narrate as:
- **The AI agent itself** ← the one used in the example below
- The bottleneck / the task that's about to be automated
- A colleague talking to their past self
- The empty chair at the presentation
- The clock

> Rule of thumb: if the narrator *could* be the CEO, you haven't picked a point of view yet.

### 2. Earn the first 3 seconds with a contradiction, not a greeting
Openings that die: "Hello team!", "We're excited to announce", "In today's world".
Openings that hold: a statement that shouldn't be true and demands the next line.
> *"I wasn't hired. I was built."*

### 3. Say the specific thing, not the category
`"saves time"` → nothing. `"used to eat your entire Thursday"` → a picture.
Swap every abstract noun for something a person can see. Time → Thursday. Cost → a chair.
Innovation → "how clever the AI behind it was."

### 4. Vary sentence length on purpose
AI scripts default to one medium-length sentence after another — that even rhythm is the
tell. Write in a deliberate pattern instead:

> **Short. Short. Then one longer line that opens the idea up and lets it breathe. Then short again.**

Read it aloud. If you never run out of breath and never pause, rewrite it.

### 5. One idea per video. Ruthlessly.
Forty seconds holds **one** thought. Everything else is supporting evidence. The example below
has exactly one: *someone on your floor built this — now go beat it.* The schedule, criteria
and prize are evidence, not topics.

### 6. Turn the CTA into a dare, a challenge, or a consequence
"Register now" is not a CTA, it's an instruction. Give people a reason that costs something.
> *"Someone on your floor is about to build the one that beats me. Make it you."*

### 7. End on a line, not a logo
The last spoken sentence is what gets repeated at lunch. Write that line first, then build
backwards to it. The logo is where the film stops, not where it lands.

---

## Part 3 · The 5-minute build recipe

1. Write the **final line** first. Everything serves it.
2. Choose a **narrator who isn't the company** (lever 1).
3. Write the **opening contradiction** (lever 2).
4. List your hard facts — dates, numbers, rules. These are **evidence**, and evidence goes in
   the middle, never the opening.
5. Draft in **~105 words** for 40 seconds. Read aloud with a stopwatch.
6. Delete every abstract noun and replace it with a thing you can photograph (lever 3).
7. Break the rhythm (lever 4). Read aloud again.
8. **Only now** describe visuals — the script decides the film, not the reverse.

---

## Part 4 · Kill list — delete on sight

| Phrase | Why it dies |
|---|---|
| "In today's fast-paced world…" | The single most AI-sounding opening in existence |
| "We're excited to announce" | Your excitement isn't the viewer's reason to watch |
| "Unlock / unleash / elevate / empower / revolutionise" | Category words with zero picture |
| "Join us as we…" | Narrating the video inside the video |
| "Stay tuned!" / "Don't miss out!" | Filler where a real reason should be |
| "Innovation is at the heart of what we do" | True of every company; therefore says nothing |
| Any sentence starting "As a leading…" | Cut it and the film gets better |
| Three adjectives in a row | Pick the one that's true |

## Part 5 · The 40-second worked example — **"THE AGENT SPEAKS"**

**The device:** the AI agent one of your own colleagues built is the narrator. It describes its
own job, then dares the room to replace it. Same facts as the standard announcement film —
totally different shape. **No dates anywhere**, consistent with the rest of this package.

### Voiceover (106 words, speed 1.05x)

```
I wasn't hired. I was built. Somebody on your floor made me in a single week.
I do the job that used to eat your entire Thursday. I don't get tired. I don't ask for a chair.
Now they want more of me. The AI Agent Building Challenge — tech and non-tech, presenting to all team members.
Three questions decide it: how much time you took back, how much cost you cut, and how clever the AI behind it was.
Two lakh for the winning tech team. Two lakh for non-tech.
Someone on your floor is about to build the one that beats me. Make it you.
```

| **On-screen text** | `> I wasn't hired.` `> I was built.` (monospace, typing live, cyan cursor) |

### Why this one is eye-catching (mapped to the levers)

| Lever | How it's used here |
|---|---|
| 1 · POV | The AI agent narrates — not the company, not HR |
| 2 · Opening | "I wasn't hired. I was built." is a contradiction, not a greeting |
| 3 · Specific | "your entire Thursday", "I don't ask for a chair", "412 → 0" |
| 4 · Rhythm | Short. Short. Long. Short. Read it aloud — it breathes unevenly on purpose |
| 5 · One idea | *Someone on your floor built this. Go beat it.* Everything else is evidence |
| 6 · CTA | A dare with a consequence, not "register now" |
| 7 · Ending | Lands on "Make it you." — the logo just holds the frame after |
