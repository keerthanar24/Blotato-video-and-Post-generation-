The India Films

Five 40-second vertical films for cross-border sellers, each built on a different transition grammar so the set reads as a campaign rather than a template. Copy any prompt straight into HeyGen.

Runtime
0:40 each · 11 scenes

Roughly 75 spoken words per film, leaving about 8 seconds of silence across the scene breaks.

Voice
Calm, warm, restrained

Speed 0.95x. Explicitly not a sales or shopping-channel read — the restraint is what carries trust.

Format
Voiceover over B-roll

No avatar on screen. Burned-in captions, 2–4 words per card, clear of the TikTok interface.

Chinese cut
Swap the lines, keep the film

Set a Simplified Chinese caption font (Noto Sans SC) or captions render as empty boxes.

Hard rule
No readable text in footage

Generated screens, labels and documents come out as gibberish, CJK worst of all. Captions carry every word.

No hero product
Plain cartons only

Each film is about a behaviour or an operating model, so nothing ties the set to one category.

01

Scroll, Then Search

India Is Moving From Search-to-Buy to Scroll-to-Buy

Feed scroll

Indian shoppers meet a brand in the feed and reach the search bar only after they have already decided.

Every cut is an upward swipe. The scrolling stops dead at scene 8.

0:409:16 · 1080×192011 scenesVoiceover-led

HeyGen prompt — English

Create a 40-second vertical 9:16 short video (1080x1920) for TikTok, aimed at cross-border e-commerce sellers considering India.

VOICE: English, female, calm, warm and restrained. Low-key and trustworthy, NOT an energetic sales or shopping-channel tone. Speed 0.95x. Let the pauses between scenes breathe.

STYLE: Photorealistic cinematic B-roll of modern India. Shallow depth of field, natural light, fine film grain, anamorphic bokeh. Warm, vibrant, premium documentary look. No avatar and no presenter on screen - voiceover only over footage. Burned-in captions, all caps, bold sans-serif, 2-4 words per card, placed in the middle-lower third, clear of the TikTok interface.

NO SINGLE PRODUCT: Never feature one specific hero product. Show many different people, many different categories, many different Indian settings. The subject is the behaviour, not an item.

SIGNATURE TRANSITION: The whole video is cut like a feed. Unless stated otherwise, every scene transitions by the entire frame sliding UPWARD out of view as the next scene slides up into place, matched to a thumb swipe. This is the film's core visual idea - the viewer is being scrolled through the story. The scrolling stops completely and permanently at Scene 8.

Build exactly 11 scenes. Use the voiceover lines VERBATIM.

SCENE 1 - 3.5s
Voiceover: "They're not looking for your product."
Caption: THEY'RE NOT / LOOKING FOR YOU
Visual: Extreme close-up of an Indian woman's thumb flicking upward on a phone in a bright Mumbai cafe. The screen is a blur of moving colour. Warm afternoon light through a window, shallow depth of field.
Transition out: The whole frame scrolls upward with her swipe.

SCENE 2 - 2.5s
Voiceover: "They're just scrolling."
Caption: THEY'RE JUST / SCROLLING.
Visual: Fast montage of four completely different people scrolling in four places - a young man on a Delhi metro, a woman in a Bengaluru office lift, a student on a Chennai rooftop at dusk, an older shopkeeper behind his counter. Every thumb moving upward in the same rhythm.
Transition: Each person scroll-wipes upward into the next, about 0.6s each.

SCENE 3 - 3.5s
Voiceover: "But the decision happens in that scroll."
Caption: THE DECISION / HAPPENS HERE.
Visual: Extreme slow motion on a single thumb swipe. Screen light sweeps across a face. Time almost stops. Dust and skin detail visible.
Transition in: Hard cut from the fast montage straight into extreme slow motion - the speed contrast is the transition.

SCENE 4 - 3.5s
Voiceover: "A short video. A review. A price."
Caption: A VIDEO. / A REVIEW. / A PRICE.
Visual: Three macro shots of a phone screen, all deliberately soft-focus and unreadable: a vertical video playing; a row of star ratings; a price area. About 1.2s each.
Transition: Upward scroll-wipe between each, snapping on the beat of the line.

SCENE 5 - 3.0s
Voiceover: "They remember it. They don't search."
Caption: REMEMBERED. / NOT SEARCHED.
Visual: The phone screen goes dark. A face lit only by the fading glow, then darkness. She sets the phone face-down.
Transition out: The screen dims to black; the next scene fades up from black.

SCENE 6 - 3.0s
Voiceover: "Days later, it shows up again."
Caption: DAYS LATER. / AGAIN.
Visual: The same feed reappearing across changing light as days pass - morning window light, midday street glare, evening lamp, blue night in bed. The same upward scroll motion in every one.
Transition: Cross-dissolve on the scroll motion so a single swipe carries through all four light changes.

SCENE 7 - 3.0s
Voiceover: "And again."
Caption: AND AGAIN.
Visual: The same upward swipe repeated five times, faster each time, a different hand and a different place each time.
Transition: Hard, fast jump cuts, accelerating.

SCENE 8 - 3.5s
Voiceover: "By the time they open the search bar..."
Caption: THEN THEY / SEARCH.
Visual: Extreme macro. A thumb taps. A cursor blinks in an empty field. Everything goes completely quiet and still. Warm light from the left.
Transition: ALL motion stops abruptly. No scroll. The film holds still for the first time. From here on there is no scrolling transition.

SCENE 9 - 3.0s
Voiceover: "...they've already decided."
Caption: ALREADY / DECIDED.
Visual: The screen light warms and blooms across her face. A small, certain smile. She taps once, decisively.
Transition out: The light blooms and overexposes the frame to pure white.

SCENE 10 - 5.5s
Voiceover: "Images. Reviews. Price. Ad placement. That's what decides if you're seen."
Caption: IMAGES. REVIEWS. / PRICE. ADS.
Visual: Out of the white bloom, a dense grid of many different marketplace-style product tiles builds across the frame - many categories, many price points, no single product favoured - until it becomes a living wall of commerce. Tiles pop in rhythmically on the beat.
Transition: From pure white, tiles snap in on the beat.

SCENE 11 - 6.0s
Voiceover: "In India, search isn't where discovery begins. It's where it ends."
Caption: SEARCH ISN'T WHERE / DISCOVERY BEGINS.
Visual: The entire wall of tiles scrolls upward and away one final time, revealing behind it a wide golden-hour shot of a crowded, thriving Indian street market that blends into a modern city skyline. Commerce everywhere, alive, full of people. Slow push-in, held.
End card fades up over the final 2 seconds: VALUECART - Be seen before you're searched.

IMPORTANT: Do not render any readable text, user interface, logos or watermarks inside the generated footage. Keep every phone screen, label and price soft-focus and abstract. All on-screen words come from the burned-in captions only.

One Door, Then Many

India Market Entry for Global Brands

Doorway pass

India looks like a single doorway. Walk through and it is a corridor of closed ones - until they open in sequence.

The camera moves through a door to cut. Light runs gold, grey, gold.

HeyGen prompt — English

Create a 40-second vertical 9:16 short video (1080x1920) for TikTok, aimed at cross-border e-commerce sellers and global brands considering India.

VOICE: English, female, calm, warm and restrained. Low-key and trustworthy, NOT an energetic sales or shopping-channel tone. Speed 0.95x. Let the pauses between scenes breathe.

STYLE: Photorealistic cinematic. Shallow depth of field, natural light, fine film grain, anamorphic bokeh. Architectural, composed, premium. No avatar and no presenter on screen - voiceover only over footage. Burned-in captions, all caps, bold sans-serif, 2-4 words per card, placed in the middle-lower third, clear of the TikTok interface.

NO SINGLE PRODUCT: Never feature one specific product or brand. Show plain cartons, pallets, and operations. The subject is the process, not an item.

CORE VISUAL IDEA - THE CORRIDOR OF DOORS: The film is built on one idea. India looks like a single beautiful doorway. Walk through it and you find a long corridor of many more closed doors, each one an operational requirement. At the end, they all open in sequence.

SIGNATURE TRANSITION: The camera passes THROUGH a doorway to cut. Each pass wipes the frame into the next scene. Light temperature is the emotional track: warm and golden at the start, cool and institutional through the corridor, then flooding back to warm gold at the end.

Build exactly 11 scenes. Use the voiceover lines VERBATIM.

SCENE 1 - 3.5s
Voiceover: "From the outside, India looks like one door."
Caption: ONE DOOR.
Visual: A single beautiful doorway standing alone in warm golden light, wide and symmetrical, perfectly composed. Beyond it, a soft blur of bright Indian marketplace colour and movement. Inviting, calm, cinematic.

SCENE 2 - 2.5s
Voiceover: "The demand is real."
Caption: THE DEMAND / IS REAL.
Visual: Fast joyful glimpses through the doorway of real Indian commerce - crowded markets, delivery bikes weaving through traffic, cartons moving hand to hand, people paying by phone. Warm, alive, optimistic.
Transition: Quick cuts on the beat, all framed through the doorway.

SCENE 3 - 3.5s
Voiceover: "Then you walk through it."
Caption: THEN YOU / WALK THROUGH.
Visual: The camera moves forward through the doorway - and the warm light drops away behind it. We are now in a long, dim corridor. Ahead, more doors. Many more, receding into the distance.
Transition: Passing through the door wipes the frame; the light shifts from warm gold to cool grey in the same move.

SCENE 4 - 4.0s
Voiceover: "Compliance. Customs. Import documents."
Caption: COMPLIANCE / CUSTOMS / DOCUMENTS
Visual: Three closed doors in fast succession, one per phrase. Cool institutional light. Between them, macro glimpses of a stamp pressing down, a seal, a form being signed - all deliberately soft-focus and unreadable.
Transition: Hard cut on each door, landing exactly on each word.

SCENE 5 - 4.0s
Voiceover: "Onboarding. Landed cost. Pricing."
Caption: ONBOARDING / LANDED COST / PRICING
Visual: Three more closed doors, faster now. Behind narrow glass panels in each door: a dim warehouse aisle, numbers of light drifting in the dark, a shifting price board. All abstract, none readable.
Transition: Hard cut on each, slightly faster than Scene 4.

SCENE 6 - 3.5s
Voiceover: "Warehousing. Fulfillment. Payments. Reconciliation."
Caption: WAREHOUSING / FULFILLMENT / PAYMENTS
Visual: Four more doors, accelerating until they almost strobe. The corridor now looks endless. Subtle dolly-zoom creating vertigo - the far end pulls away as the camera pushes forward.
Transition: Rapid cuts, accelerating to the edge of overwhelming.

SCENE 7 - 3.5s
Voiceover: "Every one of them can stop your launch."
Caption: ANY ONE / CAN STOP YOU.
Visual: All motion stops dead. One closed door fills the entire frame. A single overhead light. Total stillness - the quietest moment in the film. Dust hanging in the air.
Transition: Abrupt full stop. No movement at all.

SCENE 8 - 3.5s
Voiceover: "India isn't hard to enter. It's hard to operate."
Caption: EASY TO ENTER. / HARD TO OPERATE.
Visual: A slow, patient push toward the closed door. A thin line of warm light appears underneath it and slowly grows brighter.

SCENE 9 - 3.5s
Voiceover: "That's the difference between a launch and a business."
Caption: A LAUNCH / OR A BUSINESS.
Visual: The door swings open - and every door behind it opens too, in sequence, all the way down the corridor, one after another, warm golden light flooding in from the far end.
Transition: The chain reaction of opening doors IS the transition. Light travels toward the camera.

SCENE 10 - 3.5s
Voiceover: "One partner. One accountable path through all of it."
Caption: ONE PATH / THROUGH ALL OF IT.
Visual: A clean unbroken dolly straight down the now fully open corridor, no cuts, gathering speed toward the light. Through glass on either side the operations are alive: barcode scanners flashing, pallets moving, people working.
Transition: No cut at all - one continuous move. The only uncut shot in the film.

SCENE 11 - 5.0s
Voiceover: "India rewards the brands that arrive ready."
Caption: INDIA REWARDS / THE READY.
Visual: The camera bursts out of the corridor into wide, bright Indian daylight - a thriving market blending into a modern city skyline, cartons moving, delivery riders pulling away, commerce everywhere. Slow push-in, held.
End card fades up over the final 2 seconds: VALUECART - Ready for India?

IMPORTANT: Do not render any readable text, user interface, logos or watermarks inside the generated footage. Keep every document, label, screen and price board soft-focus and abstract. All on-screen words come from the burned-in captions only.

The Signal Was Early

The Future of E-commerce Is Predictive

Reverse time

Open on the failure, rewind through every signal that was already visible, then play forward with it caught in time.

Scenes 2-7 run backwards. Scene 8 freezes. Scene 9 snaps forward.

HeyGen prompt — English

Create a 40-second vertical 9:16 short video (1080x1920) for TikTok, aimed at cross-border e-commerce sellers and global brands operating in India.

VOICE: English, female, calm, warm and restrained. Low-key and trustworthy, NOT an energetic sales or shopping-channel tone. Speed 0.95x. Let the pauses between scenes breathe.

STYLE: Photorealistic cinematic. Shallow depth of field, natural light, fine film grain, anamorphic bokeh. Cool and clinical at the start, warming through to golden at the end. No avatar and no presenter on screen - voiceover only over footage. Burned-in captions, all caps, bold sans-serif, 2-4 words per card, placed in the middle-lower third, clear of the TikTok interface.

NO SINGLE PRODUCT: Never feature one specific product or brand. Show plain cartons, pallets, shelves, and light. The subject is the operation, not an item.

CORE VISUAL IDEA - THE FILM RUNS BACKWARDS: It opens on the failure, then rewinds through every early signal that was already visible, stops dead, and finally plays forward again with the signal caught in time.

SIGNATURE TRANSITION: From Scene 2 to Scene 7 everything moves in REVERSE - objects un-stack, vehicles reverse, dust lifts back up, motion runs the wrong way. Scene 8 freezes completely. Scene 9 snaps back to normal forward motion, and that direction reversal is the biggest transition in the film. If true reverse footage is not available, achieve the effect by ordering the shots backwards through time and using right-to-left reverse wipes between them.

Build exactly 11 scenes. Use the voiceover lines VERBATIM.

SCENE 1 - 3.5s
Voiceover: "This is the moment it costs you."
Caption: TOO LATE.
Visual: An empty warehouse bay. A clean rectangle of pale floor where a pallet stood, with a fine outline of dust around its edge. Cold overhead light. Absolutely still, absolutely silent.

SCENE 2 - 3.0s
Voiceover: "But it didn't start here."
Caption: IT DIDN'T / START HERE.
Visual: The image begins moving BACKWARDS. Dust lifts off the floor, a loaded pallet slides back into the empty bay, a forklift reverses out of frame.
Transition: Reverse motion begins here and runs continuously until Scene 8.

SCENE 3 - 3.5s
Voiceover: "Three weeks earlier, the signal was already there."
Caption: THREE WEEKS / EARLIER.
Visual: Still running backwards, faster: cartons fly up onto shelves, a scanner light un-flashes, a delivery van reverses out of a loading yard. Heavy motion blur, time peeling back.

SCENE 4 - 3.5s
Voiceover: "A region ordering faster than the others."
Caption: ONE REGION / MOVING FASTER
Visual: Still backwards. A large grid of soft light panels on a dark wall, like regions of a country rendered in light. One panel pulses brighter than all the rest. Abstract, atmospheric, nothing readable.

SCENE 5 - 3.5s
Voiceover: "A listing quietly losing its position."
Caption: A LISTING / SLIPPING.
Visual: Still backwards. A tall grid of glowing tiles on a dark wall. One tile drifts slowly downward through the grid, dimming as it goes. Quiet, easy to miss.

SCENE 6 - 3.5s
Voiceover: "Ad spend drifting toward the wrong shelf."
Caption: SPEND GOING / THE WRONG WAY.
Visual: Still backwards. Streams of light flowing through a dark space, pouring into one bright node - but running in reverse, revealing they had been feeding a dead end.

SCENE 7 - 3.5s
Voiceover: "Returns clustering in one size."
Caption: RETURNS / CLUSTERING.
Visual: Still backwards. Cartons fly back out of a returns pile, un-stacking themselves, cut tape resealing itself, the pile shrinking away to nothing.

SCENE 8 - 3.5s
Voiceover: "None of it was hidden. It was just early."
Caption: NOT HIDDEN. / JUST EARLY.
Visual: The rewind STOPS dead on a single frozen frame - one small pulsing light in a wide, dark operations space. Total stillness. The quietest moment in the film. Dust suspended in the air, unmoving.
Transition: All reverse motion halts. Complete freeze.

SCENE 9 - 3.5s
Voiceover: "Predictive isn't knowing everything."
Caption: NOT KNOWING / EVERYTHING.
Visual: Time snaps FORWARD again at normal speed. The one signal light brightens. A hand reaches into frame and touches it.
Transition: A hard snap from frozen to forward motion. The direction reversal is the largest transition in the film - make it land.

SCENE 10 - 4.0s
Voiceover: "It's seeing one signal early enough to act."
Caption: SEEING IT / EARLY ENOUGH.
Visual: Forward motion now. The warehouse comes alive - a loaded pallet moves INTO the bay before it can empty, stock arriving ahead of demand, people working with purpose, scanners flashing. Cool light warming up.

SCENE 11 - 5.0s
Voiceover: "Predictive isn't faster reporting. It's earlier decisions."
Caption: NOT FASTER REPORTS. / EARLIER DECISIONS.
Visual: Wide shot of a fully stocked, warm, fully moving Indian fulfillment operation at golden hour. Everything flowing, every bay full, nothing empty. Slow push-in, held.
End card fades up over the final 2 seconds: VALUECART - See it early. Act in time.

IMPORTANT: Do not render any readable text, numbers, charts, dashboards, user interface, logos or watermarks inside the generated footage. All data is shown as abstract light, grids and panels only. Every on-screen word comes from the burned-in captions.

Build It Or Arrive

Written to complete the set

Split timeline

Two brands enter India the same week. One builds from scratch, one arrives into an operation already running.

A horizontal split races both timelines at once, then collapses to one.
