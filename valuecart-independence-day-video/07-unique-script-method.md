# How to Write an AI Video Script That Doesn't Look AI-Generated
### The method + a worked 40-second Independence Day film ("FIFTY-TWO SECONDS")

---

## Part 1 · Why most AI video scripts look identical

Ask any tool for "an Independence Day video for our company" and you get the same film every
time: flag waving, a swell of music, "we celebrate the spirit of freedom", three values,
"Happy Independence Day". It isn't wrong. It's **shaped like everyone else's**, so the eye
slides off it — and on a day when every company posts one, shape is the only thing that
separates yours.

Uniqueness is not better adjectives. It's **one structural decision made before you write a
word.** Seven levers do the work.

---

## Part 2 · The seven levers

### 1. Pick a point of view that isn't "the company"
Biggest lever by far. Same facts, different narrator, completely different film.
For Independence Day at an e-commerce company you could narrate as:
- **The 52 seconds of the anthem** ← the one used below
- The flag itself
- The one machine that keeps running while everyone stands still
- A first-generation employee talking to their grandparent
- The building at 6am, before anyone arrives

> If the narrator could plausibly be the CEO, you haven't picked a point of view yet.

### 2. Earn the first 3 seconds with a contradiction, not a greeting
Dead: "Happy Independence Day from all of us at…", "As we celebrate…", "Today we honour…"
Alive: a statement that shouldn't be true and forces the next line.
> *"Valuecart doesn't stop. Orders move at three in the morning."*

### 3. Find the one specific number or object nobody else will use
This is what makes a patriotic film yours instead of stock. Everyone has a flag. **Nobody
else is building their film around the fact that the national anthem runs fifty-two seconds** —
and that for a company whose whole business is never stopping, those 52 seconds are the
entire idea. One concrete detail beats a paragraph of sentiment.

### 4. Vary sentence length on purpose
The even, medium-length rhythm is the AI tell. Write to a pattern instead:
> **Short. Short. Then one longer line that opens the thought and lets it breathe. Then short.**

Read it aloud. If you never pause and never run out of breath, rewrite it.

### 5. One idea per video. Ruthlessly.
Forty seconds holds one thought. Here it is: *the company that never stops, stops.* The flag,
the sweets, the team photo are **evidence**, not topics.

### 6. Make the emotional turn earn itself
Don't state the feeling ("we feel proud"). Build the conditions and let the viewer arrive
there. Motion → hard stop → silence → motion again. The meaning lands in the gap.

### 7. End on a line, not a logo
The last spoken sentence is what gets repeated. Write it first, build backwards to it.
> *"Fifty-two seconds. Once a year. Worth stopping for."*

---

## Part 3 · The 5-minute build recipe

1. Write the **final line** first.
2. Choose a **narrator who isn't the company**.
3. Write the **opening contradiction**.
4. Hunt for **one specific number or object** nobody else will use.
5. Draft at **~100 words** for 40 seconds. Read aloud with a stopwatch.
6. Replace every abstract noun with something photographable. *Unity* → a rotating chair
   stopping. *Pride* → a rope going taut.
7. Break the rhythm. Read aloud again.
8. **Only then** describe visuals. The script decides the film, never the reverse.

---

## Part 4 · Kill list — delete on sight (Independence Day edition)

| Phrase | Why it dies |
|---|---|
| "As we celebrate the spirit of freedom…" | Every company posts this exact line |
| "Let us remember the sacrifices…" | True, but it's a template, not a thought |
| "Unity in diversity" | So worn it now reads as filler |
| "We're proud to be Indian" | Stating the feeling instead of causing it |
| "Freedom is our greatest gift" | Category words, zero picture |
| "On this auspicious occasion…" | Nobody speaks like this out loud |
| Any year-count ("77th", "78th") | It's a date reference — and it ages the file |
| Three adjectives in a row | Pick the one that's true |

## Part 5 · The 40-second worked example — **"FIFTY-TWO SECONDS"**

**The device:** the Indian national anthem runs about fifty-two seconds. For a company whose
entire business is *never stopping* — orders, dispatch, dashboards, cross-border logistics —
those fifty-two seconds are the only time the whole floor goes still. The film is built
around that gap. **No dates, no year-counts anywhere.**

### Voiceover (100 words, speed 1.0x — this one needs air)

```
Valuecart doesn't stop. Orders move at three in the morning. Dashboards don't blink.
Once a year, we stop for fifty-two seconds.
No calls. No carts. No screens. Just the flag going up, and everyone underneath it standing still.
Fifty-two seconds where nobody is ops, or tech, or finance. Just people who grew up singing the same words.
Then it ends, and we go back to work — carrying Indian goods to the world, and the world's brands to India. That's the freedom part.
Fifty-two seconds. Once a year. Worth stopping for. Jai Hind.
```

### Scene-by-scene

## SCENE 1 — "DOESN'T STOP" · `0:00 – 0:06`
| | |
|---|---|
| **Visual** | Fast, kinetic, almost stressful. Time-lapse: conveyor belts, cartons sealing, a dispatch board flipping rows, a cursor tearing through a spreadsheet, headlights of a night van, a wall clock reading the small hours. Cold blue-grey grade. Nothing patriotic yet — **deliberately withhold the tricolour.** |
| **On-screen text** | Small monospace ticker, top-left: `ORDERS · LIVE` with digits rolling upward, never settling |
| **Animation** | 6 cuts in 6 seconds, each 12–18 frames, hard cuts only. Speed ramps between shots. Everything moves; nothing rests. |
| **Transition OUT** | **Hard freeze** — the frame stops dead on a mid-motion carton, colour drains 40%, audio cuts to absolute silence for 8 frames |
| **SFX** | Layered machine noise building to near-uncomfortable, then **total silence** |

## SCENE 2 — THE STOP · `0:06 – 0:11`
| | |
|---|---|
| **Visual** | Held silence. A conveyor motionless. An office chair still rotating, slowing, stopping. A phone lighting up unanswered on a desk. First warmth enters the frame: a thin band of saffron light from a window. |
| **On-screen text** | Center, thin white type, appearing letter by letter: `00:52` |
| **Animation** | Everything at 20% speed or frozen. The chair is the only motion — and it comes to rest exactly on the word "stop". The `00:52` counter begins ticking down and stays in the corner for the rest of the film. |
| **Transition OUT** | **Saffron silk wipe** left→right |
| **SFX** | Room tone only. A single distant bansuri note. |

## SCENE 3 — THE HOIST · `0:11 – 0:18`
| | |
|---|---|
| **Visual** | Low-angle hero: the rope goes taut, the flag rises briskly, then unfurls into wind in slow motion, sun flaring behind the fabric. Cut to hands on the rope. Cut to a wide of the forecourt. |
| **On-screen text** | *(none — the counter keeps ticking in the corner)* |
| **Animation** | Hoist at real speed, unfurl at 40% slow motion. Anamorphic flare blooms as the sun clears the fabric. The word "still" lands on the widest, most static frame in the film. |
| **Transition OUT** | **Flag-unfurl reveal** — fabric fills frame and pulls upward |
| **SFX** | Rope creak, wind snap in cloth, low strings enter |

## SCENE 4 — "NOBODY IS OPS OR TECH OR FINANCE" · `0:18 – 0:25`
| | |
|---|---|
| **Visual** | Slow lateral dolly along a line of colleagues standing still, backlit, faces soft. Warehouse staff in hi-vis beside desk teams in shirts — **in the same frame, same light, same posture.** That single composition is the whole scene's argument. |
| **On-screen text** | Optional small saffron chips, lower-left, appearing and dissolving: `OPS` `TECH` `FINANCE` `CATALOG` — then all four fade to a single word: `US` |
| **Animation** | One continuous 7-second dolly, no cuts — the only unbroken shot in the film. Counter ticks past `00:20`. Buntings sway behind them. |
| **Transition OUT** | **Saffron light-leak flare** |
| **SFX** | Many voices singing together, low and unpolished in the mix — never karaoke-clean |

## SCENE 5 — MOTION RETURNS · `0:25 – 0:33`
| | |
|---|---|
| **Visual** | The machine restarts, but now warm-graded and beautiful instead of stressful. The same conveyor from Scene 1 moves again. A shutter door rolls up into golden light. A glowing line-art map of India throws light trails outward across a globe, and a few arc back in. |
| **On-screen text** | **`THE FREEDOM PART`** (white, centered, saffron underline drawing left→right) |
| **Animation** | Deliberately re-use Scene 1's exact framings, re-graded warm — the audience feels the rhyme without naming it. Light trails draw on with 250ms stagger. Music reaches full swell at 0:30. Counter hits `00:00` and softly dissolves. |
| **Transition OUT** | **Tricolour particle dissolve** |
| **SFX** | Machines return, but mixed low and musical, not industrial |

## SCENE 6 — "WORTH STOPPING FOR" · `0:33 – 0:40`
| | |
|---|---|
| **Visual** | Clean endcard: deep indigo base with a soft tricolour gradient along the top edge and a faint defocused waving-fabric texture at 8%. Valuecart logo center, breathing gently. |
| **On-screen text** | **`FIFTY-TWO SECONDS.`** <br> **`ONCE A YEAR.`** <br> **`WORTH STOPPING FOR.`** (three lines, arriving one at a time) <br><br> `जय हिंद · JAI HIND` (saffron) <br> `From all of us at Valuecart` (small, grey) |
| **Animation** | Each line fades up with a 16px rise, 400ms apart, **no bounce** — dignity over energy. "JAI HIND" arrives last with a soft saffron glow. Hold the final lit frame a full 1.5s. |
| **Transition OUT** | Hold on the lit logo — **no fade to black** |
| **SFX** | Strings resolve on a warm major chord with a long tail, then room tone alone |

### Why this one is eye-catching (mapped to the levers)

| Lever | How it's used here |
|---|---|
| 1 · POV | The narrator is *the pause*, not the company |
| 2 · Opening | "Valuecart doesn't stop" — a contradiction on a day about stopping |
| 3 · Specific | Fifty-two seconds. A chair coming to rest. An unanswered phone. |
| 4 · Rhythm | "No calls. No carts. No screens." then one long breathing line |
| 5 · One idea | *The company that never stops, stops.* Everything else is evidence |
| 6 · Earned turn | Motion → hard silence → motion. The feeling lands in the gap, unstated |
| 7 · Ending | Lands on "Worth stopping for." The logo only holds the frame afterwards |

> **Fact check before you publish:** the ~52-second runtime refers to the **full version** of
> the Indian national anthem. If your celebration uses the short version, change the number
> and the title everywhere rather than letting it be almost-right.
